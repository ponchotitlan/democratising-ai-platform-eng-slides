This is AI platform engineering

The infrastructure behind running agents and their tools safely: authenticated, authorized and traceable.

  • Without it, every team solves access, tools, knowledge and approvals on its own.
  • With it, those decisions are made once, in the open, and every agent inherits them.

Why open-source AI platforming, in your own environment

Whatever your stack looks like, here is what running an open-source AI platform gives you

🏠

Runs where you are

Self-host it in your own network, cloud or cluster. Your data stays with you, and you decide what leaves it.

🔌

Plugs into your stack

Bring the models, tools and sign-in you already use, and swap any of them later. No lock-in.

🔍

Trust you can verify

Inspect how access, approvals and traces work for every agent, and debug it when it misbehaves.

🛠️

Make it yours

Adapt it to your needs, and share what you build. In open source, the community shapes the roadmap, and so can you.

The trade-off: you run it. For an AI platform, that is worth knowing up front.
Mind your bandwidth: if your team does not have the capacity to run it, a managed option may fit better.

Lessons from the field

🧱

A fresh install that failed

An image that would not pull, a service that exited at once. 7 issues filed on the CAIPE repository so far.

Lesson Budget time to run it, and file issues as you go.
🙋

A Slack user with zero tools

Created from Slack alone, they got a 403 from every tool server until a first web UI sign-in.

Lesson Identity is part of the platform. Test as a real person.
From lesson to contribution 🤝

Six traps, one proposal

Putting CAIPE behind my own domains and a tunnel cost me six traps. One service restarted about 5,900 times over a single setting.

So I proposed #3090, an official guide to running CAIPE on custom domains:

  • every URL and issuer setting, public or internal
  • one place to set the domain, not eight
  • a checklist to verify it works
Lesson Turn your scars into a proposal.

Forge at Splunk

An always-on AI assistant for Splunk’s platform engineers, built on CAIPE instead of from scratch.

🕒

The problem

Questions piled up across time zones. Alerts waited on humans. The know-how sat buried in Slack threads.

🤖

What Forge does

Answers questions in Slack and a web portal, from Slack, Confluence and docs. When an alert fires, it reads the logs, finds the likely cause and drafts a fix. An engineer approves it.

🧩

Why they chose CAIPE

Open source, so a fast start and a way to contribute back. Production AI expertise already built in. Close collaboration with Outshift.
